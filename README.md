# Men's Singles Figure Skating World Championship Scores Analysis (2005-2026)
ACC102 Track2 Mini Assignment

---

## 1. Project Overview
This project analyzes the evolution of men's singles figure skating performance at the World Championships from 2005 to 2026. It examines how total scores, technical element scores (TES), and program component scores (PCS) have changed over time, and explores the growing impact of technical difficulty on competitive results. The goal is to help fans, skaters, and coaches better understand trends in modern figure skating scoring and performance standards.

## 2. Data Source & Information
- **Primary Data Source**: Kaggle - Men's Figure Skating World Championship Scores (2005-2024)
  - Author: MITCHELL FRIESS
  - URL: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mitchellfriess/fsscores
- **Supplementary Data**: Official competition protocols (PDF) for the 2025 and 2026 World Championships, downloaded from the International Skating Union (ISU) website (https://www.isu.org)
- **Access Date**: April 2026
- **Time Period**: 2005–2026
- **Key Variables**: Year, Skater, Nation, Short Program (SP) Total/TES/PCS, Free Skate (FS) Total/TES/PCS, Number of quadruple jumps

## 3. Methodology
1.  **Data Preparation & Cleaning**: Merge Kaggle historical data with extracted 2025-2026 ISU data, check for missing values, and ensure consistent formatting.
2.  **Descriptive Trend Analysis**: Visualize overall trends in SP and FS total scores from 2005 to 2026.
3.  **Component Score Comparison**: Compare the growth of average TES and PCS scores over the years.
4.  **Correlation Analysis**: Use a heatmap to examine relationships between total scores, TES, and PCS.
5.  **Insights & Conclusion**: Summarize key trends and implications for competitive figure skating.

## 4. Key Findings
- Both short program and free skate total scores show a clear upward trend over the years.
- Technical element scores (TES) have grown faster than program component scores (PCS), reflecting increasing competition difficulty.
- Total scores have a strong positive correlation with both TES and PCS, with TES showing a slightly higher correlation.
